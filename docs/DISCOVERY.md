# Discovery and Validation

## Objective

Determine whether professional appointment scheduling between healthcare professionals and pharmaceutical / healthcare representatives is a meaningful, frequent, and underserved problem worth solving.

Discovery should challenge the product concept, not confirm it by default.

## Primary questions

We need evidence for five areas:

1. **Problem severity** — what actually goes wrong today?
2. **Frequency** — how often does the problem occur?
3. **Current alternatives** — how are appointments coordinated now?
4. **Behavioral willingness** — would healthcare professionals initiate requests through a new tool?
5. **Commercial value** — who would pay, and what outcome would justify the cost?

## Interview audiences

Prioritize a small, varied sample where feasible:

- medical/pharmaceutical representatives;
- doctors;
- midwives;
- practice/clinic administrative staff if they materially participate in scheduling;
- company managers or administrators who may influence purchasing or rollout.

The founder's spouse may provide initial workflow context only from information she is allowed to share. Do not request confidential Frezyderm information.

## Interview principles

- Ask about recent real behavior, not hypothetical preferences first.
- Prefer “Tell me about the last time…” over “Would you use…”.
- Separate frequency from annoyance.
- Look for workarounds already in use.
- Ask what happens when coordination fails.
- Avoid leading participants toward the proposed solution.
- Do not pitch the full feature set before understanding the problem.

## Suggested interview topics

### Current workflow

- How is a meeting usually initiated?
- Which channels are used: phone, SMS, messaging apps, email, secretary, CRM, calendar, other?
- Who coordinates the final time?
- How far in advance are meetings normally arranged?
- Are there informal visiting hours or recurring patterns?

### Friction

- What is the most frustrating part of arranging a visit?
- What causes back-and-forth communication?
- How often are meetings rescheduled or cancelled?
- How are last-minute changes communicated?
- What happens when someone does not receive the change?

### Healthcare-professional initiated requests

- When would a doctor or midwife want to request a representative proactively?
- Do they know which representative to contact?
- Would they choose a person, company, product/topic, or simply request contact?
- What would make using a dedicated tool worthwhile?

### Representative workflow

- How are visits planned today?
- What information is needed before accepting a request?
- Would incoming requests help or create additional workload?
- How should unavailable times be handled?

### Buyer and adoption

- Who would authorize or pay for a tool like this?
- What operational result would matter to that buyer?
- What prevents adoption by healthcare professionals?
- What rollout model would be realistic?

## Evidence grading

Use lightweight evidence labels:

- **Observed repeatedly** — multiple participants independently describe the same behavior/problem.
- **Observed once** — credible but not yet repeated.
- **Opinion / preference** — stated preference without demonstrated behavior.
- **Founder assumption** — internal hypothesis not yet supported externally.

## Exit criteria for defining the first MVP

Do not freeze the MVP until there is enough evidence to answer, at minimum:

- primary user/problem pair;
- typical initiation flow;
- minimum information required to request a meeting;
- how acceptance, decline, alternative time, reschedule, and cancellation work;
- whether healthcare professionals will realistically participate;
- likely economic buyer;
- a measurable pilot outcome.
